## Drill 02 — Latency

- **Injected** `FORCE_SLOW` on both instances at **22:23:36**; sample response times jumped to **5–6 s**.
- **`cops-alb-latency-p95` → ALARM at 22:28:25** — detection **289 s (~4 m 49 s)**.
- The p95>1.5 s alarm needs 3×60 s evaluation periods, so ~3 min is structural; the rest is ALB metric-publishing lag.
- **Recovered** by removing the flag; latency returned to normal.

## Drill 03 — Instance failure  →  FINDING: self-healing outpaced the alarm

Ran two variants:
1. **Graceful ASG termination** (`terminate-instance-in-auto-scaling-group`): the instance drained, the survivor carried the (light) load, and a replacement booted + passed health checks in ~2 min. `UnHealthyHostCount` stayed **0** the whole time — no page.
2. **Hard app stop** (`systemctl stop copsapp`): `UnHealthyHostCount` spiked to **1 in only isolated single minutes (22:41, 22:43)** — never the **2 consecutive minutes** the alarm requires — because the ASG's ELB health check terminated and replaced the failed instance faster than the alarm's window (metric evidence confirmed).

**Conclusion:** `cops-unhealthy-targets` (Max ≥ 1 over 2×60 s) is effectively un-trippable by a *single* instance failure in a 2-instance ASG — self-healing remediates first. That's arguably correct (don't page for what auto-heals), but if you *want* to be paged, the fix is one of:
- tighten to `datapoints_to_alarm = 1`, `evaluation_periods = 1` (1×60 s), or
- alarm on **`HealthyHostCount < desired_capacity`** instead (catches "we're running degraded"), or
- accept it and rely on the ASG + the `cops-alb-5xx` symptom alarm for customer impact.

## Drill 04 — RDS connection exhaustion  →  FINDINGS: threshold too high + orphaned connections

- Opened idle connections to `cops-db` at **22:49:39**; `DatabaseConnections` plateaued at **72** and the alarm (**threshold > 80**) never fired.
- **Finding 1 — threshold above the instance's ceiling:** a `db.t3.micro`'s real `max_connections` is ~80, so the exhaust maxed at 72 before Postgres refused more. The alarm can't fire before the DB is effectively saturated. **Fix:** set the threshold to a *fraction* of the actual `max_connections` (e.g. 60–70%) so it warns *before* saturation, not after.
- **Finding 2 — the pressure went customer-facing:** with connections exhausted, the app's per-request `/health` DB check began failing → targets unhealthy → the ASG cycled the instances (observed the SSM recover call fail with `InvalidInstanceId`). DB saturation cascades to the app tier.
- **Finding 3 — orphaned connections:** abruptly terminating the client instance left its **72 connections held** on the RDS side (TCP half-open) — they did **not** release on their own within the drill. **Recovery action: an RDS reboot cleared them** (`DatabaseConnections` dropped 72 → 0), restoring a clean baseline.
- **Corrective actions:** lower the threshold vs. real `max_connections`; add **RDS Proxy / PgBouncer** connection pooling so one client can't starve the fleet; give the app a bounded pool + fast-fail so DB pressure degrades gracefully instead of hanging.

## Drill 05 — DB dependency failure

- **Revoked** the `cops-rds-sg` ingress rule (cut app→DB on 5432) at **23:03:32**; `/` immediately returned **500** (DB connect timeout).
- **`cops-alb-5xx` → ALARM at 23:06:18** — detection **166 s (~2 m 46 s)**.
- **Recovered** by re-authorizing the SG rule at 23:06:24; app returned to 200. (Kept the outage window short to avoid an ASG replacement cascade.)

## Restore test  →  PASS

- **Snapshot** `cops-db-restoretest` created 23:07:12 → available 23:11:45 (~4 m 33 s).
- **Restore** to `cops-db-restore` started **23:12:21**, reached **available 23:18:57** → **measured RTO = 6 m 36 s (396 s)**, vs the **60-minute target** — comfortably inside it.
- **Validation (not just "available"):** from an app instance, connected to the restored endpoint with the master secret and ran `SELECT version()` → **`PostgreSQL 16.13`**. The data restored and is queryable.
- **Teardown:** deleted the restored instance and the snapshot.
